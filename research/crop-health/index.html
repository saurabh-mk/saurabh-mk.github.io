Crop Health

Plant disease and the products that prevent it. WILT-SHIELD tackles bacterial wilt — one of the most economically important plant diseases — from early detection to host–pathogen mechanisms and biocontrol, while the agri-biologicals programme develops biopesticides, biostimulants, herbicides and green nanoparticles with agricultural universities and industry.

Plant disease — WILT-SHIELD

  1. Early detection of Ralstonia pseudosolanacearum in planta

    WS1     Bacterial wilt affects more than 400 plant species and can cause 70–100% yield loss in chilli, eggplant, potato and tomato.     Close Details

    People

    Kristi Kabyashree (Atria) · Deepa Bhagat (ICAR-NBAIR)

    Bacterial wilt affects more than 400 plant species and can cause 70–100% yield loss in chilli, eggplant, potato and tomato. Visible wilting appears late, once the pathogen has colonised the vasculature, so we are developing a nano-biosensor system to detect it before symptoms and open an intervention window.

    Current status

    • Establishing an early-detection framework that complements symptom-based diagnosis

    Outputs

    • Research publications
    • Technology for early-stage detection of bacterial wilt

    Open to

    • Collaborations on similar projects
    • Consultancy on similar projects

  2. Host- and tissue-specific virulence in Ralstonia

    WS2     Individual virulence determinants contribute differently depending on the host and the infection route.     Close Details

    People

    Kristi Kabyashree (Atria) · Suvendra Kumar Ray (Tezpur University) · Hitendra Kumar Patel (CSIR-CCMB)

    Individual virulence determinants contribute differently depending on the host and the infection route. We identify the bacterial determinants required for host- and tissue-specific infection and relate them to host metabolic responses, building on doctoral findings of host-dependent virulence and tissue-specific colonisation.

    Outputs

    • Research publications
    • Host-specific virulence determinants of Ralstonia
    • Plant metabolites that confer resistance to bacterial wilt

    Open to

    • Collaborations on similar projects
    • Consultancy on similar projects

  3. Biocontrol of bacterial wilt with beneficial endophytes

    WS3     Beneficial endophytic bacteria can suppress Ralstonia while supporting plant health.     Close Details

    People

    Kristi Kabyashree

    Beneficial endophytic bacteria can suppress Ralstonia while supporting plant health. We identify and characterise strongly antagonistic endophytes and study how they influence pathogen virulence and plant defence, to establish them as sustainable interventions.

    Current status

    • Identifying and characterising effective endophytic candidates and their interactions with pathogen and host

    Outputs

    • Research publications
    • Endophytes and biomolecules with biocontrol potential against bacterial wilt

    Open to

    • Collaborations on similar projects
    • Consultancy on similar projects

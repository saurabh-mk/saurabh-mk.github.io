Cancer biology

  1. Serine hydrolases and their role in metastasis

    Serine hydrolases, one of the largest protein classes in the human proteome, are differentially expressed between epithelial and mesenchymal cells.     Close Details

    People

    Abhishek Sinha (lead) · Sanghamithran KS (PhD scholar)

    Serine hydrolases, one of the largest protein classes in the human proteome, are differentially expressed between epithelial and mesenchymal cells. We are identifying which of them drive epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and evaluating them as marker genes for the transition.

    Capabilities

    • Proteomics and computational-biology analysis to shortlist candidate serine hydrolases
    • In vitro confirmation of their role in EMT
    • Signalling-pathway and pathway analysis of the transition
    • Validation in animal models and patient tissue

    Outputs

    • Research publications
    • Experimental protocols for evaluating serine hydrolases in patient tissue and in vitro cells

    Open to

    • Academic and clinical collaborations for in vivo and patient-tissue expression analysis
    • Industry partnerships for development and first-line in vitro studies of potential anti-tumour drugs
    • Consultancy on similar projects

  2. Anti-tumour effects of biogenic nanoparticles and the pathways they affect

    Nanoparticles are widely studied as drug carriers and tumour-targeting agents, but poor toxicology and off-target effects limit their use.     Close Details

    People

    Abhishek Sinha (lead) · Sanghamithran KS (research scholar)

    Nanoparticles are widely studied as drug carriers and tumour-targeting agents, but poor toxicology and off-target effects limit their use. We study relatively safe, biogenic mono-metallic, bi-metallic and metal-oxide nanoparticles, and the molecular pathways through which they selectively kill tumour cells.

    Capabilities

    • Biogenic synthesis and physico-chemical characterisation of nanoparticles
    • In vitro determination of anti-tumour potential
    • RNA-seq analysis of the molecular pathways affected

    Outputs

    • Research publications
    • Patentable protocol and nanomolecules with anti-tumour effect

    Open to

    • Collaborations with academic institutions, start-ups and companies for toxicology and anti-cancer studies
    • Joint research on modifying nanoparticles as carriers or for other applications

Antimicrobial resistance

  1. Non-traditional therapeutics to eradicate dual and polymicrobial biofilms

    Decoding AMR in complex dual and polymicrobial biofilms using photoactivated organic chromophores, D-amino acids and antimicrobial peptides against rigid, persistent biofilm structures.     Close Details

    People

    Asha Velayudhan Nair (lead) · Keerthana K (PhD scholar)

    Decoding AMR in complex dual and polymicrobial biofilms using photoactivated organic chromophores, D-amino acids and antimicrobial peptides against rigid, persistent biofilm structures.

    Current status

    • Established and optimised a dual bacteria–fungus biofilm model
    • Experimental evaluation of photoactivated chromophores, adjuvants and drugs across planktonic and biofilm formats under way
    • Peer-reviewed publications in preparation

    Capabilities

    • Growth, characterisation and quantitative evaluation of planktonic versus biofilm structures
    • Screening of drugs and therapeutics as non-traditional antimicrobial candidates
    • High-throughput testing against dual and polymicrobial biofilm architectures
    • Gene regulation and expression studies by qRT-PCR

    Outputs

    • Research publications
    • Experimental protocols for evaluating non-traditional antibacterials on polymicrobial biofilms

    Open to

    • Academic and clinical collaborations for advanced biofilm modelling and mechanistic validation
    • Industry partnerships for first-line in vitro studies of antimicrobials
    • Experimental collaborations on the biological efficacy of newly synthesised chromophores
    • Consultancy on similar projects

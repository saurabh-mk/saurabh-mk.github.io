Planetary Health

Engineered microbes and enzymes that recover critical metals from waste batteries and treat hazardous industrial effluent; microbial consortia for sewage and faecal sludge; surveillance of resistance in rivers; and the ecology of insect pollinators and biodiversity. Industry-sponsored work here runs through joint centres with CrisprBits and MiniMines.

Resource recovery

  1. Bioleaching of precious metals from lithium-ion batteries

    SBR1     Microbes can leach metals from e-waste, but slow recovery undermines techno-economic feasibility on the ground. With MiniMines Cleantech Pvt Ltd     Close Details

    Radha Shivaramaiah · Upasana Mohapatra · Saurabh Mahajan

    Microbes can leach metals from e-waste, but slow recovery undermines techno-economic feasibility on the ground. We look for biological solutions that are viable, including microbial engineering approaches. The work is sponsored by MiniMines Cleantech Pvt Ltd.

    Current status

Effluent and waste treatment

  1. Biological treatment for chemical-industry effluents

    SBR2     Effluent regulation, including zero liquid discharge, makes physico-chemical treatment very costly. With CrisprBits Pvt Ltd and a specialty-chemicals company     Close Details

    People

    Saurabh Mahajan · Bapi Mandal

    Effluent regulation, including zero liquid discharge, makes physico-chemical treatment very costly. Biological treatment is limited by low efficiency and tolerance to hazardous chemicals; engineered systems can raise treatment capacity and reduce the burden on expensive and hazardous methods. We develop molecular and microbial solutions with CrisprBits and a specialty-chemicals industry partner.

    Current status

    • Metagenomic sequencing and enzyme mining from polluted areas
    • Identification of target enzymes
    • Optimisation of microbial communities

    Outputs

    • Proprietary

    Open to

    • Collaborations on similar projects
    • Consultancy on similar projects

  2. Microbial consortia engineering for faecal sludge and sewage treatment

    Isolation, identification, functional characterisation and formulation of bacterial consortia for bio-treatment of faecal sludge and sewage, improving pollutant degradation, pathogen reduction and effluent stabilisation in decentralised waste-management systems.     Close Details

    People

    Asha Velayudhan Nair · Keerthana K

    Isolation, identification, functional characterisation and formulation of bacterial consortia for bio-treatment of faecal sludge and sewage, improving pollutant degradation, pathogen reduction and effluent stabilisation in decentralised waste-management systems.

    Current status

    • Microbial strains identified from treatment plants with 16S rRNA profiling
    • Isolation and characterisation of key strains under way
    • Population data being integrated to inform consortia design

    Capabilities

    • Isolation, taxonomic identification and phenotypic characterisation of environmental isolates
    • Formulation and optimisation of functional microbial consortia for target wastewater substrates

    Outputs

    • Microbial population databases and strain-characterisation profiles for waste treatment
    • Formulated consortia protocols for faecal-sludge bioremediation

    Open to

    • Municipal wastewater authorities, sanitation start-ups and effluent-treatment organisations
    • Collaboration and consultancy on similar projects

Environment and One Health

    People

    Surveillance of river ecosystems across climatic conditions and seasons to analyse bacterial population structure, track microbial diversity and understand how resistance spreads and persists in environmental reservoirs.
